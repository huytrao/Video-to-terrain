4. RESOLUTION — scripts/kernel/kernel_da3_maxres.py, reports RESOLUTION_OK
================================================================================
The paper leaves DA3 at process_res=504 with upper_bound_resize, which scales the
LONGEST side. model_inference.py:128 maps {"high_res": "lower_bound_resize",
"low_res": "upper_bound_resize"} -- so the default is DA3's own LOW-res preset.
For 1920x1080 that predicts depth at 504x280 = 141,120 samples and bilinearly
upsamples to 2,073,600: 14.7x areal interpolation. That ceiling reaches past the
point cloud -- PGSR/scene/cameras.py:89 reads depth_da3/*.npy as TRAINING
SUPERVISION, so DA3's real resolution feeds the final surface too.

Raising it is safe from the assertion at inference_da3_scannetppv2.py:588:
api.py:352 overwrites prediction.intrinsics with the INPUT matrix before anything
compares them, so the difference is exactly zero at any resolution. The authors'
own note on the commented-out line says "this can lead to OOM issue" -- a memory
reason, not an accuracy one.

MEASURED (probes chosen to separate token cost from view cost):
  A  600 views @  504  ->  504x280     432,000 tok   peak 57.2 GiB   61 s
  B  300 views @  504  ->  504x280     216,000 tok   peak 32.8 GiB   19 s
  C  150 views @ 1008  -> 1008x574     442,800 tok   peak 59.0 GiB   63 s

A vs C at equal token count: 57.2 vs 59.0 GiB. At moderate resolution the cost is
TOKEN-DRIVEN -- views and resolution trade one for one, and cross-view attention
is NOT quadratic in the view count. That answers the open question in
docs/reference/audit-prompt-da3-resolution.md.

Escalation, resolution first, chunk size giving way:
   50 views @ 1920  ->  OOM (tried to allocate a single 7.89 GiB block)
   40 views @ 1920  ->  OOM

HONEST LIMIT OF THE FITTED MODEL: peak = 8.15 + 114.2 GiB/Mtoken fits the three

TWO OPEN RISKS WITH 20 CHUNKS -- ONE NOW MEASURED AND IT FAILED:

(a) SCALE CONSISTENCY -- TESTED, AND NATIVE-RESOLUTION CHUNKING IS REJECTED.
    scripts/kernel/kernel_chunk_scale.py infers two OVERLAPPING view sets as separate
    calls and compares the depth each produces for the frames they SHARE. Same
    frames, same supplied poses, different chunk company: if the scale fit is
    stable the ratio is 1.0.

      30 views @ 1920 (the chosen config)  median ratio 0.97995  -> 2.0% off
      200 views @ 1008 (the fallback)      median ratio 1.00113  -> 0.11% off

    The right statistic is the deviation of the MEDIAN ratio, not the worst frame:
    api.py:362 applies ONE scalar per inference call, so a chunk can only be wrong
    by one number, and the frame-to-frame spread around it is DA3's own prediction
    noise, present within a single chunk too. Gating on the worst frame would
    reject a configuration for noise chunking did not cause.

    2.0% on a ~6-unit room is ~12 cm of region-dependent scale error, against a
    +/-5 cm target -- and PGSR reads these same depth maps as supervision, so it
    would be baked into the surface. The per-frame ratios also DRIFT
    monotonically (0.990, 0.994, 0.980, 0.972, 0.966), which is a systematic scale
    difference and not scatter.

    *** CONCLUSION: native resolution with 20 chunks is NOT the highest-quality
        configuration. It is higher-resolution but geometrically inconsistent.
        The highest configuration that PASSES is process_res 1008 at 200 views
        per chunk: 4.1x the paper's real depth, 3 chunks, 0.11% scale agreement.
        That is what the production run uses. ***

(b) The paper's chunking randomises view order
    (inference_da3_scannetppv2.py:746, unseeded). Thirty RANDOM views out of 600
    spanning a 20-unit walk may barely overlap, and DA3 predicts worse depth

7. CUTTING OBJECTS OUT -- BUILT AND SELF-TESTED (src/v3d/scene/decompose.py)
================================================================================
The user asked for research into cutting objects out. The obvious route is through
semantics (VLM -> SAM3 -> lift masks) and it has three weaknesses: it can only
produce objects whose class a VLM named, it needs the gated checkpoint, and
text-prompted masks are one mask per CLASS per frame so five pallets in a row
become one region. So this takes the geometric route, which needs no model and is
therefore NOT blocked by the SAM3 gate: find the room's shell, remove it, and what
falls out in separate pieces are the objects. Semantics later LABELS them.

Three criteria decide what is shell, and all three were necessary -- each was added
because the synthetic self-test failed without it:

  1. SPAN, not flatness. A machine housing and a wall are both planar; only the
     wall spans the room. Without this the flat side of every crate is deleted.
  2. HALF-SPACE removal, not inliers. A reconstructed wall has thickness, so its
     far surface is a second plane outside any sane tolerance. Deleting only the
     near face left the far one as a large flat phantom "object": 27 objects where
     there were 3.
  3. BOUNDARY position. A plane must sit at the scene's extreme along its own
     normal. Without this, a plane fitted across the room's middle was classified
     as a wall and its half-space deleted a crate standing behind it.
     Threshold 0.06, chosen between two MEASURED populations: the six real shell
     planes sit at edge 0.012-0.031, a thin panel 1 unit in front of the far wall
     sits at 0.13. At 0.15 the panel was absorbed and deleted -- and a factory is
     full of that shape (cage panels, signage, machine guards).

Two guards on half-space removal, both from the same test: no single plane may
claim >45% of the mesh, the accumulated removal may not exceed 90%, and if
half-space removal leaves under 2% of the mesh it is rejected wholesale in favour
of inlier-only removal. The two errors are not symmetric -- keeping phantoms is
recoverable and the audit flags them, deleting every object is not.

SELF-TEST: scripts/sim/selftest_decompose.py, reports SELFTEST PASS.
A room built with up = +Y (so no hardcoded axis can pass by luck) holding a crate,
a cabinet, and a deliberately thin panel. Measured:

  object          centre error    extent error
  panel           0.044           12%
  cabinet         0.056           33%
  crate           0.025           25%
  up axis         [-0.0, 1.0, -0.0], derived from the cameras
  room height     3.104 vs true 3.000
  audit           5/5 PASS
  height map      0.18% holes, MJCF compiles, 1000 steps stable

Centres are accurate; extents run 12-33% large. On this test that is partly
legitimate -- the synthetic room is built from SOLID boxes, so shell removal
correctly shaves the faces touching floor and walls, which a TSDF surface does not
have. Treat extents as approximate until checked against real annotation.

A BUG IN THE TEST ITSELF, worth remembering: matching found objects to true ones by
sorted extent pairs the smallest with the smallest, which compared a panel fragment
against a crate and reported "100% error" about the wrong pair. Match by CENTRE;
extent is the thing being measured and must not also decide what to compare.

ALSO FIXED IN THE TERRAIN EXPORT: height_map sized its surface sampling from the
triangle count, giving roughly one sample per grid cell and 58% empty cells on a
CLOSED room -- holes that are an artefact of undersampling and read downstream as
pits a robot falls into. Sample count is now a ratio to the grid (16 per cell):
58.49% -> 0.18%.

   the mislabelling behind the 8.6 m room, in new code. Unit now comes from the
   document's scale mode.

C. _boxes_video looked for size_m/extent; the schema is box.size/box.center. It
   would have found zero boxes, silently fallen back to the geometric cut, and
   labelled a semantic run "geometric". Now raises rather than falling back.

D. The @step decorator executed each step at DEFINITION time and the steps were
   also called explicitly, so all seven ran twice -- doubling render and GPU cost
   while merely looking slow.

Also: turntable_video takes a PATH and reads it with read_triangle_mesh, which
returns an EMPTY mesh for a point-cloud PLY -- a blank orbit with no error. The
point cloud now has its own renderer.
Also: libx264 rejects odd dimensions; a frame sized from an aspect ratio is odd half
the time (1280x1275 here). encode() now crops to even and rejects an empty output.
Also: MuJoCo's offscreen framebuffer defaults to 640x480 and must be declared in the
MODEL (<visual><global offwidth/offheight>), not passed to the renderer.

13. NATIVE-RESOLUTION DEPTH: ATTEMPTED TWICE, MEASURED, REJECTED
================================================================================
Native 1918x1078 is 14.65x the paper's real depth per frame against the 4.1x this
run shipped, and it fits in memory at 30 views per call. It is still not usable, and
the reason is not memory.

Every model.inference call fits its own Umeyama scale and divides its depth by it
(api.py:353-362). Twenty calls means twenty scales. scripts/kernel/kernel_da3_native.py
tried to remove that by running ONE reference pass over all 600 views at 504 (57.2
GiB, globally consistent by construction) and correcting each native chunk by a
single scalar fitted against it.

Measured by scripts/kernel/kernel_verify_native.py, which reads only the depth maps on disk:

  estimator                        cross-chunk excess over the within-chunk floor
  median-of-medians                          +9.11 points
  matched-pixel (area-pooled)                +6.92 points
  tolerance                                   1.00 point

The second estimator is the right one -- comparing a median over 1918x1078 against a
median over 504x280 compares different pixel populations, and native resolves thin
and near structures the low-resolution pass never sees. Fixing it helped by 2.2
points and did not come close.

WHY IT CANNOT WORK, from the in-chunk ratio spread diagnostic: for 14 of 20 chunks
the per-frame native/reference ratio varies by 0.7-2.3% (near the 0.86% noise floor),
so one scalar is the right correction. For 6 chunks it reaches 6-27%. Where the
spread is 27% the discrepancy is NOT a per-call scale -- the model predicts different
relative geometry at 14.65x the resolution, not merely rescaled geometry -- and no
per-chunk scalar can remove it.

The 20 M-point native cloud was DELETED rather than delivered. It would have been the
most impressive artefact in the set and was measured at ~7% region-to-region scale
inconsistency.

If someone wants to try again: the two axes are chunk composition (contiguous gives
good view overlap and badly-conditioned scale fits; spread gives the opposite) and
chunk size (only 30 views fit at native resolution, which is what forces the choice).
A per-frame rather than per-chunk correction is NOT the answer -- it would flatten
genuine depth variation between frames.

FIVE THINGS THAT WERE WRONG FIRST, AND HOW EACH WAS CAUGHT

1. DOUBLE-REPRESENTED MACHINES -- and this is why the earlier MJCF never settled.
   97% of the height map's tall interior cells sit under a detected object's box:
   the machines ARE the terrain. The previous export added 43 object boxes on top,
   i.e. inside solid ground -- 63 overlapping pairs, peak |qvel| 48.8. Representing
   each machine once fixes it at the root. --objects-as-bodies is the other
   consistent choice (carve them out AND add hull bodies) for manipulation work.

2. WALLS COLLAPSED 7 -> 1. Offsets were compared without dividing by the normal's
   component or subtracting the grid origin, so every plane looked like one edge.
   Projected properly they are u=0.321, u=5.967, v=0.119, v=5.869 -- a closed room.

3. A FIXED WALL BAND MADE THE DOORWAYS DECORATIVE. +/-9 cells left 0.24 units of
   wall standing in front of two of three openings. The band is now measured from a
   MEDIAN across-wall profile (median so a machine parked against a wall cannot drag
   the band across the room): 0.26-0.38 units. All three openings then read clear.

4. THE SPAWN LANDED OUTSIDE THE BUILDING. Removing the wall bands made the strip
   *behind* each wall the flattest, most open ground on the map, so it scored best.
   Fixed by bounding the search to the interior. The world is then shifted so that
   cell is the origin, which means the robot's own `home` keyframe (35 values,
   pelvis 0.841) places it correctly -- and survives a plain qpos0 reset, which a
   rewritten keyframe would not.

5. FOUR MEASUREMENT BUGS IN MY OWN CHECKS, each of which would have been reported
   as a scene defect:
   - MuJoCo CACHES ASSETS BY FILENAME WITHIN A PROCESS. Three different heightfield
     orientations gave RMS identical to 0.01 mm because only the first was ever
     read. Orientation must be measured one process per candidate.
   - A ray cast down from the origin hits the ROBOT'S HEAD (1.718), not the floor.
     Probes now skip every geom that is not terrain/wall/partition/object.
   - The doorway probe ran AFTER 2 s of uncontrolled simulation, when the robot is
     sprawled across the floor; it measured the robot's shoulder. Reset first.
   - The camera-placement probe used +forward when MuJoCo puts the camera at
     `lookat - distance * forward`. It probed the opposite side of the room, called
     it clear, and rendered the inside of a wall. Twice.

HONEST LIMITS OF THIS SCENE
  - Lengths are DA3 UNITS. DA3 is metric, so 2.307 is its own ESTIMATE of the
    ceiling, not an arbitrary number -- but unverified, and low for a factory. One
    tape measurement sets --metres-per-unit and rescales every length, mass and
    force. Masses and forces are meaningless until then.
  - 20.7% of the terrain is INTERPOLATED floor (the wall bands). The floor under a
    wall was never seen by the camera. Filled from nearby FLOOR cells, not nearest
    cells -- nearest-fill borrowed machine heights and left a ledge along the wall.
  - The partition is INVENTED. It is the only way to satisfy "divide the factory"
    from a reconstruction that is one room. It is named partition* so it can never
    be mistaken for evidence.
  - The three reconstructed doorways lead OFF THE HEIGHTFIELD -- there is no
    reconstructed floor outside the room. Walking out of one means walking off the
    map. The partition doorway is the one that connects two real halves.
  - Doorway SIZES are chosen (1.20 x 2.05), not measured. Only their POSITIONS come
    from the data, because the same lift3d pass that produced them under-segmented
    the floor badly enough to fail Stage J and called a 0.42 fragment a door.
